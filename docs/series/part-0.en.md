# Rewriting the Planet's OS: Series Guide and Contents


We already know a great deal about the climate crisis. Scientists have warned us for decades, millions of people have raised their voices, and enormous sums have been spent. Yet fossil-fuel use keeps rising, and carbon pricing has been rolled back again and again.

This series does not blame a lack of effort. It argues that we have tried to cut emissions **through effort alone, while leaving untouched the core rule of the economy: the more you expand, the more you gain.** Over eight parts, it draws a blueprint for rewriting that rule: **Planetary OS**.

---

## What this series describes

Planetary OS is a set of operating rules that keeps society running within the planet's physical limits. Its aim is **a world where reducing pays**.

Past efforts have run into two barriers:

- **Barrier 1: the burden falls only on the present.** Higher prices hit today's households directly, and public support erodes.
- **Barrier 2: reducing alone means losing.** For individuals, firms, and nations alike, cutting back unilaterally is a loss.

Planetary OS builds in, from the start, the conditions needed to overcome both.

| Module | Role |
|---|---|
| 1. Stock accounting and BEI | Those who benefited most from past emissions pay a thin, broad levy, managed as a lasting endowment (the PTE) |
| 2. SAS | A one-way cap on fossil fuels at the upstream point, with allowances sold at auction |
| 3. Settlement and return | 90% of auction revenue goes directly to citizens. People who use less receive more, and a majority-benefit rule tops up households just short of break-even so that at least 70% of people come out ahead |
| 4. Tamper-resistance and rule amendment | A "double key" of scientific justification and approval by citizens' representatives prevents backsliding |

The target is **staying well below 2°C**. Instead of waiting for a world government, Planetary OS spreads through **a club that is worth joining and costly to stay out of**.

All names, ratios, and figures in this series are **illustrations**, meant to show a direction. The actual design should be set through scientific review and democratic deliberation.

## The eight parts

### Part 1: Why Hasn't All This Effort Changed the World?
[Part 1: Why hasn't all this effort changed the world? →](part-1.md)

So many resources and so much goodwill, so little change. Why? Not because humanity lacks ability or goodwill, but because our energy has gone to the wrong address, and because no one has drawn a blueprint for what comes next. Beneath that lies a deeper cause: an economy whose rules reward expansion.

### Part 2: Planetary OS: The Big Picture
[Part 2: Planetary OS: the big picture →](part-2.md)

The whole blueprint, laid out as a single map: the four conditions a new OS must meet, the open assembly that writes it (the PPA), and the four modules and two money flows. It also answers the worry "isn't this a controlled society?"

### Part 3: SAS: A Cap That Only Moves One Way
[Part 3: SAS: a cap that only moves one way →](part-3.md)

A cap on fossil fuels that falls automatically every year. Part 3 resets the target from 1.5°C to "well below 2°C," asks why a cap rather than a price, and explains how this can succeed where the EU Emissions Trading System could not. The key is two barriers, and two conditions that overcome them.

### Part 4: Giving Future Generations a Voice
[Part 4: Giving future generations a voice →](part-4.md)

Those most affected by the climate crisis cannot yet vote. Four mechanisms help democracy represent both the present and the future: a Future Generations Council, "virtual future citizens," a global citizens' assembly chosen by lot, and a Future Trust for every child.

### Part 5: Not Sacrifice, but Gain: Designing a Dividend That Rewards Using Less
[Part 5: Not sacrifice, but gain →](part-5.md)

How the money goes back to society. Why return it to citizens rather than governments? Can it be measured fairly without surveillance? How much money is it? In a high-income country, the illustration comes to about $4,000 a year for a family of four. It also covers rewards for firms and regions, and possible future "sufficiency action points."

### Part 6: Scaling Without a World Government
[Part 6: Scaling without a world government →](part-6.md)

If one country caps emissions alone, its industries move elsewhere. Part 6 solves the prisoner's dilemma between nations with a club that pays to join and costs to stay out of. Reduction rates rise with club size, rules reward early movers, an endowment built on historical liability provides investment and guarantees, and high-income countries gain too.

### Part 7: Why Climate Campaigns Keep Losing: From Petition to Implementation
[Part 7: From petition to implementation →](part-7.md)

Carbon fee and dividend is not a new idea. Part 7 looks at six weaknesses shared by the efforts that failed, and proposes shifting the movement's energy from petitioning politicians to implementation: building a working system first and showing it.

### Part 8 (final part): The First Step: A Blueprint, and the Young People Who Carry It
[Part 8: The first step →](part-8.md)

How can one idea grow into a movement that engages the world? Part 8 introduces the Youth-Led Global Citizens' Congress for Future Generations (Youth GCC), three first steps, four stages from blueprint to implementation, and what you can do after reading.
