Citizen Dividend Simulator

An upstream cap on fossil fuels raises prices. Under Planetary OS, 90% of the auction revenue comes straight back to you, with a top-up from those who benefited most from the past. See what that means for your household.

Every figure here is illustrative. The model is deliberately simple, and its ratios (90% returned, split 60/40, a 10% top-up) are examples from the proposal, not final values. Before any guarantee, the share of people who come out ahead is, as a rough calculation, from about 60% to the low 70s depending on the assumptions. On top of that, the standard design includes a majority-benefit rule: households just short of break-even receive a top-up so that at least 70% come out ahead. These are model estimates, not promises of what will happen in practice. Change the inputs to see for yourself.

Revenue is price × emissions. As the cap tightens, emissions fall faster than the price rises, so the dividend eventually shrinks. That is the system working. The Planetary Transition Endowment (PTE), funded separately by BEI, provides the lasting base.

How the numbers are calculated

  1. Auction revenue per person = allowance price × average footprint per person (all fossil CO₂ is assumed to be covered by the cap).
  2. Your extra costs = price × your footprint × share passed on to households. Only these direct costs are counted; indirect effects through wages, pensions, and the wider economy are not modeled.
  3. Universal dividend = 60% of the share returned to citizens (54% of revenue at the 90% default), plus the launch-phase top-up from the PTE.
  4. Sufficiency-linked dividend = 40% of the share returned (36% of revenue at the default), shared by a score: 75% weight on low use (2 − your use ratio, capped at 0–2) and 25% on cutting back (1 + cut ÷ 20%, capped at 0–2). Scores are scaled so the pool is paid out exactly.
  5. Children receive the same dividends as adults. Half of each child's dividend is paid to the parents now, to cover the higher prices the household pays for the child; the other half is saved in the child's Future Trust account. Under the majority-benefit rule (standard: at least 70%), households just short of break-even receive an extra top-up from the PTE equal to their shortfall, starting with the smallest shortfalls, until the chosen share comes out ahead. The amount is fixed by each household's use before cutting back, so cutting back still pays. The PTE's total top-up is capped at 40% of revenue.
  6. Use across the population follows a log-normal distribution with mean 1×. Under a cap, spending the dividend cannot raise total emissions; it only raises the allowance price.
